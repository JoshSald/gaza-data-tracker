Situation Update
Gaza Strip
• Evacuation order and general health updates
• Five evacuation orders were issued between 5 and 12 October, covering areas in Bureij and Nusseirat camps in
northern Deir Al Balah; Bani Suhaila, Shaikh Nasser and Ma'an in Khan Younis; Gaza and North Gaza.
• One medical point is within the evacuation area announced on 12 October, in addition to one primary healthcare
center (PHC) and six medical points within 420 meters of the zone.
• A total of 61 health facilities (20 in North Gaza and 41 in Gaza), including 10 hospitals, 19 PHCs, and 32 medical points
are within the evacuation zone announced on 6 October, ordering approximately 431,000 people residing in Gaza and
North Gaza to evacuate to Mawassi area in the South.
• One primary healthcare center (West Nusseirat PHC) is within the evacuation area announced on 5 October in
northern Deir Al Balah, while 12 health facilities, including Al-Awda Nusseirat Hospital (420m), are within one
kilometer of the affected area.
• OCHA reported that, as of 15 October, at least 75,000 people have been displaced over the past 10 days, mostly within
the north, with around 100 people having crossed from northern Gaza southward via Salah Ad Din or Al Rashid roads.
• The deteriorating security situation in North Gaza is hindering access to health services in the governorate, and
increasing the burden on facilities in Gaza City to respond to the acute health needs of the vulnerable population. All
primary healthcare centers in north Gaza are currently out of service, while access to the three hospitals is
compromised.
• On 14 October, Al-Aqsa Hospital reported at least four people killed and 40 injured in an attack on the tents of
internally displaced people inside the hospital.
• The attack on Rufaida School in Deir Al-Balah, on 10 October, hit the medical point at the school and resulted in the
death of two nurses out of the total 28 people killed, in addition to 6 health workers injured out of the 56 reported
injuries in the attack.
• Persistent fuel shortages continue to threaten humanitarian interventions, including health care provision,
impacting the functionality of hospitals, PHCCs and ambulance services.
• Medical evacuation of patients outside of Gaza remains limited since 7 May. As of 15 October, out of 15,600 cases
requested for medical evacuation, 5,138 (33%) have been evacuated, since October 2023, with only 229 patients
evacuated since Rafah Closure in May 2024. Of utmost urgency is the restoration of medical evacuations from Gaza to
the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, where hospitals are ready to receive patients. Patients must also be
facilitated to be transferred to Egypt and Jordan, and from there to other countries when needed. WHO appeals for the
establishment of multiple medical evacuation corridors to ensure sustained, organized, safe and timely passage of
patients via all possible routes, including Rafah and Kerem Shalom.

• According to Israeli authorities, over 1,200 Israelis and foreign nationals were killed inside Israel, and approximately
5,400 reported injuries. As of 15 October, there have been 353 reported fatalities and 2,345 reported injuries of Israeli
soldiers since the start of the ground operation in the Gaza Strip. One hundred and one hostages remain in Gaza.
• Polio
• The second round of the polio vaccination campaign began on 14 October, targeting an estimated 591,719 children
under 10 years old with a second dose of the novel oral polio vaccine type 2 (nOPV2). Phase I will run from 14–17
October, targeting 179,160 children, Phase II from 19–22 October, with a target of 293,275 children, and Phase III is
tentatively planned for 23–24 October, pending further coordination, to reach 119,279 children. Humanitarian pause
zones from Round 1 remain in place for Phases I and II, though adjustments may occur in Phase III due to the recent
security developments.
• A total of 337 vaccination teams and 135 supervisors will be involved in Phase I, which will take place in Central Gaza.
A cumulative of 156,943 children were vaccinated (88% of phase I target population), including 128,121 children
between two- and 10-years receiving vitamin A supplements.
• Malnutrition
• The Nutrition Cluster reported that a total of 350,405 children were screened for malnutrition, from January to 12
October 2024. Out of this, 24,089 (6.9%) were admitted for malnutrition treatment, including 5,214 (1.5%) with severe
acute malnutrition, and 18,875 (5.4%) admitted due to moderate malnutrition.
• Three severe acute malnutrition (SAM) stabilization centers (SC) are fully functioning across the Gaza Strip,
supported by WHO and MedGlobal at Patients Friends Benevolent Society Hospitals in Gaza City (10 beds each); WHO
and IMC at the IMC Deir Al-Balah FH (7 beds); and the third supported by MSF-Spain at Nasser Medical Complex in
Khan Younis (6 beds). Accessibility to the only SAM SC serving the North Gaza governorate at Kamal Adwan Hospital
(10 beds) is compromised, due to the security situation. Two children with SAM with complications who were admitted
to Kamal Adwan SC defaulted and were displaced with their families.

Response
• WHO has procured more than USD 35.1 million worth of health commodities, 56% of which has already been delivered
to the Gaza Strip. Procured items include essential trauma emergency supply kits (TESK), non-communicable diseases
and SAM kits, in addition to critical medications and supplies that are needed to maintain provision of essential health
services.
• WHO and partners continue to carry out missions, support patient transfers, conduct needs assessment, support
deployment of emergency medical teams (EMT) and field hospitals, and deliver medical supplies to health facilities,
across the Gaza Strip.
• The Health Cluster reported that 75 partners are currently operational across the oPt, reaching a weekly average of
377,100 people with various healthcare services and supplies.
• WHO delivered essential medications and supplies to the Ministry of Health (MOH) and different health partners,
including UNRWA, As-Sahaba Hospital, the Palestinian Medical Center, MSF-Belgium, Project Hope, Abdel Shafi
Community Health Association (ACHA), which are enough to provide health services to 119,200 patients with
non-communicable diseases.
• WHO provided MOH and health partners, including ACHA, Project Hope and MedGlobal, with essential medications to
manage 11,000 patients with mental health disorders.
• In total, 243 health service points, including 135 medical points, are currently operating throughout the Gaza Strip,
providing essential primary health care services to the vulnerable population. Mapping of population density and
health service points availability is ongoing to identify gaps in service delivery.
• Inter-cluster reception points are activated on Salah Ad-Din and Al-Rashid streets, to register internally displaced
people crossing from Gaza to the south. Water, food, and basic stabilization health services are provided, while cases
requiring further interventions are referred to the proper service provider.
• The Health Cluster and WHO regularly update the oPt Unified Health Dashboard, providing a snapshot of
conflict-related casualties, communicable disease surveillance, medical evacuations, attacks on health, and overall
Health Cluster response in the oPt, with emphasis on the ongoing emergency in the Gaza Strip.

• On 12 October: WHO conducted a mission to As-Sahaba Hospital in Gaza City, to deliver 23,000 litres of fuel, medical

• On 12 October: WHO led a joint mission and transferred 19 critical patients from North Gaza to Shifa Hospital in Gaza
city. The team also delivered 20,000 litres of fuel to Kamal Adwan (15,000) and Al Awda (5,000) hospitals, to maintain
service provision at the hospitals.
• On 14 and 15 October: WHO conducted missions to areas in Deir Al-Balah to deliver the poliovirus vaccine and
logistical support needed to implement the first phase of round II of the poliovirus campaign
WHO Recommendations
• Immediate ceasefire.
• Expand and sustain humanitarian access into Gaza for fuel, water, food, medicines and other necessary supplies.
• Expand humanitarian corridors and ensure safe passage to allow delivery of humanitarian aid and personnel within
Gaza.
• Active protection of civilians and health care, preserving the function of remaining health facilities.
• Establish and strengthen a routine process for medical evacuation for all patients in need, without distinction of any
kind, ensuring timely referral of the +10,000 critical patients that need to be medevaced out of Gaza.
• Tactical pauses in the north to enable polio vaccination campaigns.
